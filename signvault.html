Overview

Making SignVault easier to use

SignVault is CBRE’s existing platform for managing sign orders, users and inventory. As one of its administrators, I used it every day and ran into its usability problems firsthand.

I took that experience into my own redesign exploration of the product: reorganizing how it’s navigated, simplifying the everyday order and inventory tasks, making dense operational data easier to read and adding reporting. The result is a working prototype that covers every main area.

What I owned
  • Identifying friction from daily use as an administrator
  • Restructuring navigation and the core order and inventory tasks
  • Visual design for data-dense tables, filters, status and reports
  • Directing the redesign and prototyping it with Claude, with light and dark themes
What changed

The decisions behind the redesign

Navigation

Eight links nested under a single “Work Orders” menu became five clear sections in a persistent sidebar: Dashboard, Users, Orders, Inventory and Reports.

Workflows

Separate View All, View Open, View Completed and Advanced Search pages became one Orders page, with category and status filters and the map built in.

Information hierarchy

The dashboard went from an order table paginated across 189 pages to headline counts first, then orders by category, top submitters and recent activity.

Reusable patterns

Page headers, tables, filters, status and category badges and actions share one set of styles, and one green palette replaces the orange-and-green mix.

Responsive and themed

Dashboard, orders and reports reflow for small screens, keeping counts, filters and the map usable, and every screen works in light and dark themes.

Takeaway

Clarity for an information-heavy tool

SignVault shows how I approach complex products: start from where the structure gets in people’s way, regroup the navigation and tasks around how the work is actually done, and carry one consistent visual system through every screen, so dense operational data stays clear on any device.
