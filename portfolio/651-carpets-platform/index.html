Overview

Four systems, one platform

651 Carpets ran its business across four disconnected systems, covering scheduling, customer management, estimating, product management and invoicing. Employees moved between applications to finish routine tasks.

The goal wasn’t just a fresh coat of paint. It was one platform that kept everything each system did while making everyday work far easier to navigate. The project paused before launch when business priorities shifted, but it remains one of the most valuable product challenges of my career.

What I owned
  • Mapping how information flowed between the four systems
  • Information architecture and navigation for the combined platform
  • Interface design across CRM, scheduling, estimating, products, invoicing and reporting
  • An 80-screen interactive prototype in Adobe XD
  • A refreshed logo and a lightweight design system
Key decisions

Reducing complexity without removing functionality

Designed as one system

I mapped the flow between scheduling, customers, estimates, products and invoices, then grouped related work instead of designing each feature on its own.

Shallower navigation

One sidebar and fewer levels replace four separate apps, aiming to reduce navigation depth, unnecessary clicks and duplicate data entry.

Familiar patterns

Where it helped, I borrowed proven conventions from products like Shopify to shorten the learning curve.

Lightweight design system

Shared color, type, spacing, buttons, forms and navigation made the interface easier to learn and ready to grow.

Brand refresh

An updated logo with an orange “+” for the move into more flooring and countertops, an Iris Blue palette and Acumin Pro for data-heavy screens.

Brand and design system

A refreshed identity

The redesign extended beyond workflows to a more modern identity that reflected the company’s growth beyond carpet installation.

Logo

The existing brand was recognizable but dated, so I introduced a refreshed identity that kept its familiarity while creating a cleaner, more contemporary foundation. An orange “+” represents the company’s expansion into additional flooring and countertop products.

Color and typography

To maintain familiarity, I retained elements of the existing brand. The refreshed “Iris Blue” palette provides continuity with the company’s identity, and Acumin Pro was chosen for its clarity and readability across data-heavy screens.

Design system

A lightweight design system covered color, typography, spacing, buttons, forms, navigation and common UI components. These standards made the interface easier to learn, reduced visual noise and created a foundation that could scale as functionality was added.

Takeaway

Systems thinking at product scale

651 Carpets shows how I handle a messy, real-world operation: map how the work actually flows, keep what people rely on, and turn several disconnected tools into one coherent product with a consistent visual system.
