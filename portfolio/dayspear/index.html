Overview

Running a team shouldn’t take five different tools

DaySpear is a self-initiated product that brings employee scheduling, shift coverage, time-off requests, team communication and daily tasks into one connected experience for small businesses, without the complexity of traditional workforce-management software.

The idea came from conversations with my daughter about working in restaurants. Schedules went out late, coverage was sorted out through calls and group texts, and it was hard to see who was available or who had asked for time off. I set out to design a lightweight platform that brings those everyday responsibilities together, so it’s easier to plan the week, respond to changes and keep everyone informed.

What I owned
  • Product strategy and feature definition
  • Workflows for owners, location managers and employees
  • UX/UI design across desktop and mobile
  • Brand identity: logo, color, type and pattern
  • Marketing website design
  • An interactive prototype, built with AI-assisted development
Product strategy

One connected experience

Make the important things visible

Coverage gaps, scheduling conflicts and pending requests surface right where managers can act on them.

Cut the back-and-forth

Requests, conversations and tasks connect directly to the people and shifts they affect.

Design for each role

Owners and managers get the tools to coordinate a team; employees get a focused, mobile view of their own day.

Scheduling

A clearer view of the week

The schedule gives managers one view of shifts, hours and staffing across every location, or one location at a time. Color-coded roles, weekly totals and inline flags separate the routine from what needs attention: open shifts, time off that clashes with a shift, and changes that haven’t been published yet. Managers build the week by clicking and dragging, then publish when it’s ready.

Time off and coverage

Turning requests into decisions

Each pending request shows the shift it would affect, so managers see the conflict before they decide. A single action approves the time off and opens that shift for coverage, and every request links back to the schedule. Swaps and coverage requests have their own tab, with recent decisions alongside.

Communication

Conversations in context

Messages follow how small teams actually work: announcements, a channel for everyone and one per location, direct messages, and a conversation for each shift that needs coverage. Managers can pin an announcement, send it to a single location and see who has read it, while coverage discussions stay attached to the shift they’re about.

Daily operations

Connecting shifts to the work

Tasks take DaySpear beyond the schedule. One-off to-dos can be assigned to a person, a shift or a location and managed as a list or a board, while checklists cover routines such as opening and closing.

Employee experience

Different roles, different needs

Managers need the full picture; employees need what affects their day. The mobile view leads with the next shift, its checklist and the latest announcement. From their phones, employees can flag a shift they can’t make, request time off, chat about a shift and pick up open shifts, and only shifts at their own locations that don’t clash with their schedule or time off are offered. Rather than shrinking the desktop interface, it’s organized around a different set of priorities.

Brand identity

A brand built around momentum

DaySpear’s identity balances the energy of a busy workplace with the calm of a well-organized day. The brandmark combines a D with a forward-pointing spearhead, a nod to direction and purposeful action, and it carries through the product, the marketing site and the employee app.

Color

A vibrant green paired with deep forest tones keeps the identity approachable but confident. A darker green ink handles text and links, while pale green and neutral surfaces keep busy screens calm.

Typography

Poppins carries headlines, labels and numbers. Inter handles the interface and body text, where legibility at small sizes matters most.

Pattern

A repeating pattern built from the shapes of the brandmark adds texture to the marketing site without competing with the content.

Marketing website

Bringing the product to market

The marketing site turns DaySpear’s features into a clear story for small-business owners. Rather than opening with a feature list, it leads with familiar problems: building the schedule, filling open shifts and keeping the team informed. Product previews, approachable photography and a live preview of the product show how DaySpear fits into an ordinary week.

Where it stands

From scattered tools to one product

DaySpear is an ongoing independent product. I led the product strategy, branding, experience design and iterative refinement, using AI-assisted development to turn product decisions into a working, interactive prototype that you can explore as a manager or as an employee.
